How our estimates work

Every CostMyProject calculator starts from the same question a contractor asks: what exactly is the scope? You enter your project's real dimensions — square feet, linear feet, window count, system size — and the calculator prices materials, labor and the line items homeowners usually forget (tear-off, permits, disposal, code-required extras) using current 2026 pricing data. Labor is the swing factor in almost every home project: it typically runs 40–60% of the total, and it varies by region, which is why our guides break out labor separately instead of hiding it in one lump number.

Each estimate is shown as a range, not a single figure, because real quotes vary with material grade, site conditions and your market. Use the low end for basic materials and straightforward installs, the high end for premium finishes or difficult access. Our figures are budgeting estimates to help you plan and compare contractor quotes — not bids. When a contractor's quote lands far outside the range, the breakdown on each page shows you which line item to ask about. Read our full methodology for how prices are sourced and updated.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a home project cost in 2026?

It depends on scope and region. A mid-range kitchen remodel typically runs $25,000–$55,000, a full HVAC replacement $7,500–$14,000, and a roof replacement $9,000–$22,000 for an average home. Each calculator on this site prices your exact project instead of a national average.

Are these calculators really free?

Yes — every calculator and guide is free, with no email signup and no quote-request forms. The site is supported by advertising, not by selling your details to contractors.

Why do contractor quotes differ so much from estimates?

Most gaps come from scope: permits, tear-off and disposal, code upgrades and material grade are often excluded from a first quote. Our breakdowns include those line items, so you can compare quotes on equal terms.

Should I DIY or hire a professional?

Painting, basic flooring and landscaping are common DIY wins. Electrical panels, gas lines, structural work and roofing usually require licensed pros — and often permits. Our guides flag which parts of each project are safely DIY-able.
